## What the Home Office caseworker guidance lists

The Skilled Worker caseworker guidance is written for Home Office staff, and its section on translating documents says what a caseworker should expect. If the documents are not in English or Welsh, the applicant must provide a fully certified translation from a professional translator or translation company that can be independently verified by the Home Office.

The same section lists four things the translation must include:

- confirmation that it is an accurate translation of the document
- the date of translation
- the full name and signature of the translator, or of an official from the translation company
- the contact details of the translator or translation company

The guidance also says that if no translation is supplied, the caseworker should ask for one. If the applicant still does not provide it, or the translation cannot be verified, the document will not be accepted and the application is processed as if it had not been provided. Read the page for your own route before you order anything.

The general gov.uk page on applying to come to the UK gives the shorter version: provide certified translations of any documents that are not in English or Welsh. This is not immigration advice: check the rule for your own application.

## What the NMC, GMC, HCPC and UK ENIC ask for

Maria is a made up example: a Bulgarian nurse with a diploma, a birth certificate and a marriage certificate, applying to join the nursing register. She goes down the table, ticks the rows that apply to her and crosses out the bodies she is not applying to.

For the NMC, the page on preparing your evidence says that for every document not in English you provide the original, a photocopy of the original stamped and signed by a translation service, a complete and accurate English translation also stamped and signed by the translation service, and the contact details of the translator or translation service. The page adds that the NMC cannot accept the document without these. The same page is the one to read for a nursing application, and our guide on [English lessons for a nursing registration resit](/blog/oet-or-ielts-lessons-for-a-nursing-registration-resit/) covers the language side of that route.

For the HCPC, the translations page says each document not in English needs a translation, that each translation needs certifying, and that the translator should be a professional authorised in your country to translate official documents, usually with a stamp or seal. It adds that a British Embassy, Consulate or High Commission may help you find a translator if you apply from abroad.

For UK ENIC, which issues a Statement of Comparability for international qualifications, the documents page lists certified translations among what you upload. Its Translation Waiver Service lets you submit documents in the original language for the languages it covers, and it does not provide translations. For other languages, the page says UK ENIC accepts certified translations from professional translators. We did not find Bulgarian named on the pages we read, so check the list for your language.

For the GMC, the page on translating documents that are not in English did not load for us. Read it on gmc-uk.org on the day you book, and take nothing from us about what it asks.

## Documents going the other way, to Bulgaria

If a UK document is going to a Bulgarian authority, the receiving rules are Bulgarian. The Bulgarian Ministry of Foreign Affairs civil status page, read on 21 November 2026, describes one route for a birth record. A legalised record goes to the municipality with a certified Bulgarian translation made by a Bulgarian mission or a translation company, and the translator's signature is certified by a Bulgarian notary. The page also says an apostille under the Hague Convention applies unless a legal assistance treaty waives legalisation.

We have not read a treaty position for the UK, so we say nothing about one. Ask the consulate or the municipality what they accept, and check the Ministry page for your document. Sworn translation and legalisation for Bulgaria are arranged on request, and the quote confirms the steps for your document.
