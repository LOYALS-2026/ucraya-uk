The gov.uk family visa page asks for at least A1 on the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) for your first application. At the extension after 2.5 years it asks for A2 in speaking and listening if you passed A1. A B1 or higher result can be reused at settlement. Exemptions exist, so check your own case.

## The three stages: entry, extension, settlement

A partner visa usually has three points where English matters. We read the gov.uk page on [knowledge of English for family visas](https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa/knowledge-of-english) on 21 November 2026, and the table below follows it. It is a timeline of what the page lists, not advice for your case.

| Stage | Level the page lists | What to remember |
|---|---|---|
| First application | At least A1 | You may choose to take a higher level test |
| Extension after 2.5 years | A2 in speaking and listening, if you passed A1 first | An earlier A2, B1, B2, C1 or C2 result can be used again |
| Settlement after 5 years | B1 or higher can be reused | The test must still be on the approved list |

Rules for immigration applications change, and the page you read on the day matters more than any guide, including this one. We cannot give immigration advice. If your case is complicated, ask an adviser who is regulated to give it.

## A1 at entry

The page says that if you have no qualification that proves your English, you need to pass a Secure English Language Test (SELT) with an approved provider. For a first visa application it asks for at least level A1. You can choose a higher level test, and the page notes that a result at B1 or higher can be used again at settlement.

You may not need a test at all. The page lists several groups who do not need to prove their English, including people over 65, people with a physical or mental condition that prevents them meeting the requirement, and nationals of the countries and territories it names. It also lists a degree from a UK institution, or a degree taught in English from an institution outside the UK, as another way to prove your English. If you already proved your English in an earlier successful visa application, the page says you may not have to prove it again.

The [gov.uk guidance on Secure English Language Tests](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) says partner applications need a test that assesses speaking and listening. It names the providers on the approved list, and we read it on 21 November 2026. Compare the current list on that page before you book anything.

## A2 at the first extension

The page says you may have to pass a higher level if you want to stay after 2.5 years, and that it depends on the level you passed for your first visa.

- If you passed A1, you need at least A2 in speaking and listening.
- If you passed A2, B1, B2, C1 or C2, you can use the test result again, as long as the test provider has not withdrawn your certificate.
- If you were given an exemption for your first application, you need to pass a test at A1.

The page also says that if you have been in the UK on a family visa for 5 years and you are extending as a partner or parent, you do not need to prove your English. Read the page for how that applies to you.

This is why some people choose a higher level at the start. Passing A2 or B1 first time may mean one test fewer later, but only if the result is still valid and on the approved list when you need it. That is a planning question to check on gov.uk, not something a lesson provider can decide for you.

## B1 at settlement

For settlement after 5 years, the page says that a result at B1 or higher from your earlier application can be used again. Your test must still be on the approved list, and your certificate must not have been withdrawn by the test provider.

If you did not take a B1 test earlier, you would need to meet the requirement for settlement in another way. The route page, not this guide, tells you what that is.

## What may change from 26 March 2027

Here we report only what we read, because this is the part most likely to change.

The statement of changes to the Immigration Rules published as HC (House of Commons) 1691 on 5 March 2026 contains many entries that set a minimum level of B1 in speaking and listening for applications made before 26 March 2027 and B2 for applications made on or after that date. We read the [HC 1691 text](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1619-5-march-2026/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1691-5-march-2026-accessible-version) on 21 November 2026. One group of entries is headed Changes to Appendix Settlement Family Life and uses the same two dates.

We cannot tell you whether that appendix is the settlement route for your partner visa. The family visa page we read for this guide says a B1 or higher result can be used again at settlement, and it does not mention B2. The Trinity College London page for [B1 Graded Examinations in Spoken English (GESE) Grade 5](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/B1-GESE-grade-5) lists Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR) applications made before 26 March 2027 under B1, which fits the same date change. If you plan to apply for settlement after that date, read the current gov.uk guidance for your route before you book a test.

## Choose a test, prepare and check gov.uk

Pick the test from the approved list, not from an advertisement. The gov.uk SELT page warns that some third party providers claim links with the Home Office when they have none, so book through the provider's own site. A test certificate from a language school, including ours, does not count as the test.
