It depends on the date you apply and on your route, not on the date of your test. A government paper says some applications made on or after 26 March 2027 need B2 instead of B1. Check your own route on gov.uk first, then work back from your application date to plan the test and your practice.

## What HC 1691 says about 26 March 2027

House of Commons paper (HC) 1691 is a Statement of Changes to the Immigration Rules, laid before Parliament on 5 March 2026. The Immigration Rules are the rules UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) follows when it decides applications. We read the [accessible version on gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1619-5-march-2026/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1691-5-march-2026-accessible-version) on 21 November 2026.

The English levels use the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). B1 is an intermediate level and B2 is the next one up. In several places, the paper sets two wordings side by side:

- Where the date of application is before 26 March 2027, the level is B1.
- Where the date of application is on or after 26 March 2027, the level is B2.

The paper makes this change in many parts of the rules. Examples we read include the settlement wording for the Skilled Worker and Scale-up Worker routes, and the shared Appendix English Language. It also says the test must be a speaking and listening test taken at an approved Secure English Language Test (SELT) centre no more than two years before the date of application.

We are not lawyers and this is not immigration advice. The paper is long and technical. Use it to see that the date exists, then read the gov.uk guidance for your own route.

## Applications made before and after the date

The word that matters is application. The paper ties the level to the date the application is made. It does not tie it to the date you sit the test or the date you start lessons.

A made up example: two people take the same B1 speaking test in the same week. One applies in February 2027 and the other applies in April 2027. On the paper's wording, the first application falls before the date and the second falls on or after it. Where B2 applies to the second person's route, a B1 result would be the wrong level for that application.

This is why two sets of advice can both sound sensible and still disagree. Advice that says B1 is enough may be right for an application made before the date. Advice that says B2 may be right for one made after it. Ask which date the advice is about, and which route.

## Is your route covered? Check before you plan

Not every route moves to B2. Trinity College London, a test provider, carries a notice on its [B1 test page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/B1-GESE-grade-5). We read it on 21 November 2026. The notice is the provider's own, not the government's. The notice says most people applying for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR), also called settlement, will need a B2 speaking and listening test from 26 March 2027. It lists three routes that stay at B1: British National (Overseas) (BN(O)), Armed Forces and Domestic Worker (private household). The notice adds that no changes apply to other immigration routes.

Treat that as a pointer, not as the rule. The rule is on gov.uk. Start at the [gov.uk page on proving your knowledge of English](https://www.gov.uk/english-language), which we read on 21 November 2026. The page lists two ways to prove English: an English qualification at B1, B2, C1 or C2 level, or a degree taught or researched in English. The page also notes that some people do not need to prove it, and warns that your application will be refused if you send the wrong qualification.

Here is a short route check to fill in:

| Question | Where to look |
|---|---|
| Which route am I applying under? | Your visa documents |
| Does my route need an English test at all? | The gov.uk guide for your route |
| Which level applies to the date I will apply? | The same gov.uk guide, read on the day |
| Is my route one of those Trinity lists as staying at B1? | The Trinity page, then confirm on gov.uk |

If you are unsure, ask UKVI or a regulated immigration adviser. We cannot say which level you need.

## A month by month booking timeline

Here is the timeline to copy. It works backwards from an application date. The weeks are planning choices, not rules. The only rule in it is the one from HC 1691: the level follows the date of application, and the test is taken no more than two years before it.

A made up example: Ana plans to apply in February 2027 on a route where she has checked that B1 applies to an application made before 26 March 2027.

| Month | What Ana does | Before 26 March 2027 | After 26 March 2027 | Check your route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| November 2026 | Reads the gov.uk guide for her route and writes down the date she read it | Level written down: B1 | Level written down: B2 where it applies | Confirm her route on gov.uk |
| December 2026 | Books the speaking test and starts lessons | Book a B1 test | Book a B2 test only if the route needs it | Confirm the test is on the approved list |
| January 2027 | Sits the test and keeps the result safe | Test within two years of application | Same two year wording | Confirm again before applying |
| February 2027 | Applies, after re-reading the gov.uk guide | Application falls before the date | Not applicable to Ana | Re-check the level on the day |
| After 26 March 2027 | Not applicable to Ana | Not applicable | A later application is judged under the later wording | Re-check your route |

Leave some slack. Test slots can fill up and results take time, so a plan that finishes on the last possible day has no room for a retake.

## Why the application date decides what you book

Some people wait because a higher level feels far away. Others rush to book because they think the date is a deadline for everyone. Neither is a safe habit.

If your route moves to B2 and your application falls on or after the date, a B1 result taken earlier does not meet the level the paper sets for that date. If your route stays at B1, as Trinity's notice says some do, waiting for a B2 plan costs you time and money for nothing. Either way, the first step is the same: confirm the level for your route and your planned application date on gov.uk.

Two practical points follow from the paper's wording. First, a test result is only usable for a limited time before you apply, so booking very early is not always an advantage. Second, if your plans move, the level can move with the date, so re-read the guide whenever you change your application date.

## What B1 speaking and listening preparation involves

If your route needs B1, the Trinity page describes its B1 test, Graded Examinations in Spoken English (GESE) Grade 5, as a 10 minute speaking and listening test with an examiner. It has two parts: a topic you prepare in advance, and a short conversation on two subjects the examiner chooses. Other providers may offer a B1 test for your route, so check the list on gov.uk.
