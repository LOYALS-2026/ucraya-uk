Bulgarian citizens abroad register the marriage in Bulgaria. The Bulgarian Ministry of Foreign Affairs page gives two routes: send a certified copy to the Bulgarian consulate within six months, or take the legalised certificate with a certified Bulgarian translation to the municipality. Check the page for your own case.

## Why register, and the six month rule

A marriage that took place in the UK is recorded by the UK registry. Bulgaria asks its citizens to report it as well, and the Ministry's [civil status page](https://mfa.bg/en/services-travel/consular-services/consular-services-abroad/civil-status) deals with this under the heading of registering Bulgarian citizens' civil marriages contracted abroad.

The page says that Bulgarian citizens residing abroad are under the obligation to bring or send a certified copy or excerpt of the marriage certificate to the Bulgarian diplomatic or consular mission in the relevant country, together with an application indicating their permanent address in Bulgaria. It sets a limit of six months after the local civil registry authority draws up the certificate.

The page then describes an alternative: citizens may bring the legalised marriage certificate directly to the relevant Bulgarian municipality, with a certified translation into Bulgarian. We read the page on 21 November 2026. Rules and wording change, so read it again before you act, and ask the consulate if your situation is unusual. This guide is not legal advice.

## Getting the right UK marriage certificate

Start with the certificate itself. The gov.uk page on [getting a document legalised](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised) says the Legalisation Office can legalise documents issued by a public registry, such as a marriage certificate. It also says other documents can be legalised if a UK public official, such as a notary or solicitor, has certified them.

Before you order or send anything, ask the Bulgarian consulate or municipality what kind of copy they need: the full certificate or an excerpt, and whether a certified copy is enough. Then check that both names, the date and the place are spelled correctly. A mistake on the certificate is much easier to fix before you start than after the apostille is attached.

## Apostille first, translation second

An apostille is a stamped certificate that confirms the signature, stamp or seal on a UK document. The Ministry page says that a certificate must be legalised through an apostille under the Hague Convention unless the state that issued it has a legal assistance treaty with Bulgaria that explicitly waives legalisation. We have not checked whether a treaty applies to UK certificates, so ask the consulate or the municipality whether an apostille is needed in your case.

If it is, the gov.uk page lists the standard paper-based service at £45 per document plus courier or postage costs, and says it usually takes up to 25 working days plus courier or postage time (page read on 21 November 2026: [get a document legalised](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised)). The page also says you cannot get an e-Apostille for marriage certificates or other documents from the General Register Office, so check which service your certificate can use.

The order matters. The Ministry page says the certificate is accompanied by a certified translation into Bulgarian, and the translator works from the document you will hand in. Do the apostille first, so the translation covers the final document. The Ministry page says the translation is made by a Bulgarian diplomatic or consular mission or by a translation company that the Ministry has a contract with, and that the Bulgarian translator's signature is certified by a Bulgarian notary. Ask the consulate or municipality who they will accept before you order.

## Names after marriage: what to check

The Ministry page does not discuss names, but names are where papers most often fail to match. Compare how each name is written on the marriage certificate, in the passport and on the Bulgarian identity document. Look at the order of names, any middle names, and the spelling when a Cyrillic name is written in Latin letters.

If the documents differ, do not guess. Ask the consulate or the municipality what they need to see, and tell the translator in advance, so the translation follows the spelling they expect.

## Handing in the papers

The Ministry page gives two places to hand in the papers. The first is the Bulgarian diplomatic or consular mission in the country where you live. The second is the Bulgarian municipality. The page says the municipality is the one where the permanent address of the spouse who is a Bulgarian citizen is, and where both spouses are Bulgarian citizens, it names the husband's permanent address.

Here is a made up example, a checklist for Ivan and Sarah, who married in Leeds. Ivan is a Bulgarian citizen, and Sarah is not. Use it to see the pattern, then replace the details with your own.

| UK step | Bulgarian step | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| Order the marriage certificate from the registry and check both names | Ask the consulate or municipality what copy and which steps they need | Ivan and Sarah |
| Send the certificate to the Legalisation Office, if an apostille is needed | | Ivan |
| | Order the certified Bulgarian translation of the legalised certificate | Translator, ordered by Ivan |
| Compare Ivan's name on the certificate, passport and identity card | Hand in the papers and the application with his permanent address in Bulgaria, at the consulate within six months, or at the municipality | Ivan |

A mismatch in a name could lead to questions at the registry, so the comparison is a step in its own right.
