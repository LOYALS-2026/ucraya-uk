It depends on the gap and the calendar. If one skill is a little short and your university accepts a retake of that skill, rebook quickly. If several skills are short, a short block of lessons before the next full test is usually better. Count the days to your Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS).

## Two questions before you rebook

Before you pay for anything, answer two questions on paper.

1. **What exactly does the university need, and by when?** Copy the English condition from your offer letter: the test name, the overall score, any minimum for a single skill, and the date it needs your result. Our guide to [what to do when you miss your offer score](/blog/missed-your-ielts-offer-score-what-to-do-before-the-cas/) covers how to read it.
2. **Which test version does the offer name?** The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) has versions, and the wrong one can be a wasted test. Our guide on [IELTS Academic or UKVI](/blog/ielts-academic-or-ukvi-which-test-does-your-offer-ask-for/) shows how to tell, where UKVI means UK Visas and Immigration.

This guide is about the step after both questions are answered: retake now, or study first. It does not repeat those two guides.

## Work out the gap: one skill or several

Write your four skill scores next to the four scores you need: Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. Subtract. The shape of the gap decides most of the choice.

- **One skill short, by a small margin.** This is where a retake of that skill can make sense, if it is accepted.
- **Two or more skills short.** A full retake is likely, and one more attempt without changes in how you practise is a risk.
- **Overall score short, but no single skill far below.** Check whether your university set a minimum for each skill as well as the overall score, because it changes which skills matter.

Also ask yourself why the score was low. Nerves, timing and unfamiliar question types are quick to fix. A weak range of grammar or vocabulary takes weeks. If you do not know the reason, the first lesson should find it out (see the last section below).

## Test dates and results time against your CAS date

The IELTS page on [One Skill Retake](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/booking-your-test/one-skill-retake), read on 23 November 2026, gives these points:

- Your original test must have been an IELTS on computer test, and the retake must be within 60 days of the original test day.
- You can retake once per original test.
- Results of the retake come within 3 to 5 days.
- For UKVI, the page lists One Skill Retake as accepted for IELTS Academic and General Training tests on computer in selected locations.
- Before booking, the page tells you to check with the organisation whether it accepts One Skill Retake.

The page does not give a results time for a full test, so ask your test centre when you book. Add the days for a full retake: a place at the centre, the test day, the wait for the result, and sending the Test Report Form to the university. Then compare the total with your CAS date. A CAS is the document your course provider issues so that you can apply for a Student visa, and the gov.uk page on [knowledge of English](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/knowledge-of-english) (read on 23 November 2026) describes the English requirement for that visa. Your visa route is your own to check there.

## A lesson block first, or a retake at once

**A short lesson block.** If the calendar allows, a short, focused block usually beats open-ended study. Plan it backwards from your test date.

- Spend the first lesson finding the cause of the lost marks, from your skills report and a short timed task.
- Spend the middle lessons on the one or two skills that are short, with feedback after every attempt.
- Keep the last lesson for a timed practice in the order of the real test.

Keep the block small and specific. Five or six focused lessons on a named weakness are easier to schedule and to judge than a vague plan to study more.

**When retaking at once is the sensible choice.** Retaking at once is the sensible choice in three cases. First, the deadline is so close that lessons would not finish before the last test date. Second, the cause was clearly a bad test day, such as illness or a technical fault, and your practice scores are above the target. Third, only one skill is short and admissions have said in writing that they accept One Skill Retake.

In the first case, you can still use short sessions between booking and test day, but the test date comes first.

Here is the decision table. It is a guide for thinking, not advice about your own case, so check each step against your offer and the pages above.

| Situation | Suggested next step |
|---|---|
| One skill is 0.5 short | Ask admissions in writing about One Skill Retake. If yes, book it and use a few lessons on that skill before the date |
| Two skills are short | Plan a full retake, with a lesson block first if the dates allow |
| Overall score is short | Check whether each skill also has a minimum, then plan lessons for the lowest skills |
| Deadline is under three weeks | Book the earliest suitable test first, then use short sessions on the weakest skill before the day |

Here is a made up example with made up dates, for a student we will call Priya. Her university needs her result by 20 December. Her first test has Reading 6.5 and Writing 5.5, and she needs 6.0 in each skill. Only writing is short, so she asks admissions, who confirm in writing that One Skill Retake is accepted. Her original test day was 14 November, so the 60 days run to 13 January. She books a retake for early December, has four writing lessons in between, and keeps a gap of several days before 20 December for results. If admissions had said no, her plan would change to a full retake, so she would book a place first and start lessons the same week.

## What to ask a tutor in the first lesson

Bring your skills report, your offer condition and your dates. Then ask:

- What lost me the marks in the weak skill, and how long does that take to fix?
- Is a retake of that skill enough, or do I need a full test?
- What should I do between lessons, and how will we check it worked?
- If my test date is close, what is the most useful use of the last week?

A good tutor will answer plainly, including when the honest answer is that there is not enough time. We do not run IELTS and cannot promise a score. Tell us your test date and your weak skill when you ask, and we will suggest a plan for the weeks you have.
