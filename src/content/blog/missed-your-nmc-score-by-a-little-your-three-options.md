You have three routes, and they come in order. First see whether two Occupational English Test (OET) sittings can be combined. If not, retake the whole test. The employer route is only for a narrow miss in one part, after combining has been tried. The Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) sets the rules, so read its pages.

## Find which part you missed

Get your OET certificate and write the grade for each part next to the NMC line. We read the [NMC page on OET](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/accepted-english-language-tests/oet/) on 21 November 2026. It lists at least grade B (350 or above) for reading, listening and speaking, and at least grade C+ (300 or above) for writing.

Most people who miss are short in one part. Write down which part, and by how many grades. That one fact decides which of the three routes is open to you. If your result is IELTS (International English Language Testing System) and not OET, the routes are the same in shape, but the scores differ, so use the [NMC page on accepted tests](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/accepted-english-language-tests/) for your own numbers.

## Combining two sittings: the 12 month and two year rules, and the floor scores

The NMC page lets you combine scores across two sittings of the same test. We read the rules on 21 November 2026, and they say:

- the test dates (not the certificate dates) must be within 12 months of each other;
- both tests must be no more than two years old when the NMC assesses your complete application;
- you must be tested in all four parts each time;
- between the two sittings you need one pass score in each part; and
- you can only combine tests of the same type, so no OET with IELTS.

The OET page adds that OET@Home can be combined with OET on Paper or OET on Computer, and that you should choose the Nursing version of the test. Check the page before you book, because the details can change.

The rule that surprises people is the floor. The NMC OET page says every score across both sittings must be at or above a minimum. For reading, listening and speaking the minimum is grade C+ (300 to 340). For writing it is grade C (250 to 290).

So a very weak result in one part of one sitting can block you, even if the other sitting is strong. The NMC page gives its own example of this: a listening grade of C in one sitting is below the C+ minimum, so that combination is not accepted.

Before you book a second sitting, you can try the NMC test combining calculator, which the page says you can use as often as you like. It asks you to enter the scores for both tests, and it says it does not store your information or link it to your application.

## A made up decision tree: Anna's calendar

This is a made up example. Anna is not a real person and the dates are invented. She is a ward nurse who sits OET on 10 March and misses the writing grade. Her other three parts meet the NMC line, and her writing grade is C, which is above the writing floor.

Here is the tree she follows.

| Question | Anna's answer (made up) | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| Is the first sitting the same test type as the next one? | Yes, OET again | Combining is possible |
| Is the first result at or above every floor? | Yes, writing is C and the others are above C+ | The first sitting can be used |
| Can the next sitting be within 12 months of 10 March? | Yes, 20 September is six months later | Inside the 12 month limit |
| Will both test dates be under two years old when the NMC assesses her application? | Yes, if the application is assessed within two years of 10 March | Inside the two year limit |
| What must the second sitting show? | At least C+ in writing, and C+ or better in the other three parts | Then the pair can meet the NMC line |
| What if the second sitting misses a floor? | The pair is not accepted | Retake, or ask about the employer route |

The calendar is the part people forget. Anna has to count from the date she sat each test, not the date on the certificate, and she has to keep her application on a timetable that lets the NMC assess both results while they are still within two years. The exact sums depend on your own dates, so work them out with the NMC calculator and the NMC page open.

## Retaking the whole test

If the pair cannot be combined, or you would rather start clean, you can sit the test again from the beginning. A retake means all four parts. The NMC page says you cannot take or retake individual parts separately.

A fresh sitting that meets every line in one go needs no combining at all. It also gives you a new test date, which resets the two year clock. The cost is time and another test fee, which you pay to the test provider, so look at the provider's own page for the current fee on the day you book.

Whichever route you choose, a retake or a second sitting is easier to plan if you know which skill let you down. Put a mock paper in your calendar a couple of weeks before the real one, so a weak mock still leaves time to work on the gap.

## The employer evidence route and who it suits

The NMC page on narrow misses says that if you have exhausted the combining options and narrowly missed the score in only one part, you may be able to use additional information from your current UK employer, depending on your circumstances. The NMC calls this supporting information from employers (SIFE).

We read the NMC SIFE pages on 21 November 2026. They say SIFE is supplementary, so it can only go alongside other English language evidence. For a test miss, they say you may be eligible if you have attempted two English language tests and narrowly missed the score in one part by no more than half a grade in OET (or 0.5 in IELTS). They also list work conditions: a non-registered role in a UK health or care setting for at least 12 months full time or the equivalent, with the same employer for that time. The decision to provide SIFE is the employer's, and the employer can say no.

That is a lot of conditions, so this route suits a small number of people. We cannot tell you whether you qualify. Read the NMC SIFE page for applicants and ask your employer and the NMC.

## Planning a resit around your work calendar

Whichever route you take, put the dates on one page: your first test date, the latest date a second sitting can fall, and the day you expect to apply. Mark your shifts and leave out the weeks you know you will be tired.
