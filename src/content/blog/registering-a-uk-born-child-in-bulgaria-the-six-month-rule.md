Citizens abroad must bring or send a certified copy or excerpt of the foreign birth record to the Bulgarian mission within six months, with an application giving a permanent address in Bulgaria. The alternative is a legalised record with a certified Bulgarian translation, taken to the municipality. Read the Ministry page and ask the office you will use.

## The six month duty for citizens abroad

The Bulgarian Ministry of Foreign Affairs has a page on civil status abroad. We read it on 21 November 2026. For a birth abroad, the page says Bulgarian citizens living abroad are under a duty to bring or send to the Bulgarian diplomatic or consular mission in the country, within six months after a local authority draws up a birth record, a certified copy or excerpt of it. The application must give their permanent address in Bulgaria.

This guide says what the page says and tells you where to check. It is not legal advice, and the page can change.

Here is a made up example countdown. A baby is born in the UK on 3 March, and the birth is registered with the UK registry on 10 March. The six months run from the record, not from the birth, so in this example the latest date is 10 September. The dates are only an illustration.

| 10 March | The birth is registered in the UK and the record is drawn up. Order the certificate and mark 10 September as the latest date |

## Step one: the right UK birth certificate

The Ministry page talks about a certified copy or excerpt of the local birth record for the mission route, and a legalised birth record for the municipality route. It does not say whether a short or full UK certificate is wanted, and we did not read that anywhere. Ask the consulate or municipality before you order, because a second certificate costs time.

The gov.uk page on legalisation says you can get a birth certificate from a public registry legalised, and that you should check with the person asking for the document whether they need the original or a certified copy. That second question is yours to put to the Bulgarian office.

## Step two: the apostille from the Legalisation Office

An apostille is a stamped certificate that confirms a document's signatures, stamps or seals match official records. In the UK, the gov.uk page says the Legalisation Office does this.

The standard paper apostille is £45 per document, plus courier or postage costs ([the gov.uk page on getting your document legalised](https://www.gov.uk/get-document-legalised)). We read the page on 21 November 2026, so check it on the day you apply. The page says the standard service usually takes up to 25 working days, plus courier or postage time, and you will see the current time frame when you apply.

The page also says you cannot get an e-Apostille for birth certificates or any other General Register Office document, so a birth certificate goes through the paper route. If the Bulgarian office wants an apostille, this is the step to plan around.

On Bulgaria's side, the Ministry page says that a record issued in a state which has no legal assistance treaty with Bulgaria waiving legalisation must be legalised, by an apostille under Hague Convention No. 12 or by the other route the page describes. It also says a record does not need legalising if a treaty waives it. We did not read a UK treaty position, so we say nothing about whether one applies. Ask the consulate or municipality, and see the Hague Conference site, which the Ministry page points to.

## Step three: the Bulgarian translation and its certification

On the municipality route, the Ministry page says the legalised birth record must come with a certified translation into Bulgarian. According to the page, a Bulgarian diplomatic or consular representative or a translation company makes the translation, and a Bulgarian notary certifies the Bulgarian translator's signature.

On the page, the marriage section words the translation company condition differently, mentioning a contract with the Ministry. We do not claim our translator meets any particular condition. Ask the consulate or the municipality what they accept, and tell your translator the answer.

## Embassy or municipality: where to hand it in, and what comes after

There are two routes on the Ministry page, and you choose between them.

- **The mission route.** You bring or send the certified copy or excerpt to the Bulgarian mission in the UK within six months, with the application giving your permanent address in Bulgaria.
- **The municipality route.** You bring the legalised record, with the certified Bulgarian translation, directly to the municipality. The page names the municipality of the mother's permanent address, or the father's if the mother is not a Bulgarian citizen.

After registration, the page says every newborn child receives a Personal Identification Number (PIN), called the ЕГН in Bulgarian. The municipality issues a Bulgarian birth certificate with the PIN entered in it. The parents then submit a passport application for the child after obtaining that certificate. Delivery of the foreign document to the municipality can be in person, through an authorised person, or through the Bulgarian missions abroad.
