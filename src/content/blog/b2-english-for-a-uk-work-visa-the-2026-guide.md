Usually yes. The gov.uk page on knowledge of English for the Skilled Worker visa says you must show you can read, write, speak and understand English to at least B2 on the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR). People who held the visa before 8 January 2026 and are extending it need B1. Exemptions exist, so check your case.

## What the page says now

We read the gov.uk page on [Skilled Worker knowledge of English](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/knowledge-of-english) on 21 November 2026. The page says that if you have no qualification that proves your English, you need to pass a Secure English Language Test (SELT) with an approved provider. A SELT is a test taken with a provider on the government's list, so lessons can prepare you but never replace it.

The page asks for B2 in all four skills: reading, writing, speaking and understanding. B2 is an upper intermediate level. A B1 test that checks only speaking and listening is not the same thing, so look at which skills your route asks for before you book.

Other ways to prove your English are listed on the same page: a UK school qualification such as a General Certificate of Secondary Education (GCSE) or an A Level begun before the age of 18, a degree from a UK institution, or a degree taught in English from an institution outside the UK. The page also lists nationals of certain countries and territories, and doctors, dentists, nurses, midwives and vets who have already passed an assessment accepted by their professional body. It adds that you may not have to prove your English again if you proved it in an earlier successful visa application.

We cannot give immigration advice. The page, not this guide, decides your case, and rules change.

## Who needs B2 and who keeps B1

This table follows the gov.uk page we read. It is a summary of what the page says, not advice for your case.

| Your situation | Level the page lists | Proof again? |
|---|---|---|
| New application | B2 in reading, writing, speaking and understanding | Yes, unless an exemption applies |
| Had the visa before 8 January 2026, now extending or updating | B1 | No, you do not need to prove it again |
| Switching from a Health and Care Worker visa | Not stated as a level | No, you do not need to provide proof again |
| Switching from a different visa | B2 | Yes |

The switching rows are easy to mix up. The page treats a switch from a Health and Care Worker visa differently from a switch from any other visa, so find the row that matches the visa you hold today.

## Which tests can prove B2

The gov.uk page on [Secure English Language Tests](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) says the Skilled Worker route needs a test that assesses reading, writing, speaking and listening. We read it on 21 November 2026. It names these providers and tests on the approved list:

- IELTS SELT Consortium: IELTS for UKVI (the International English Language Testing System, for UK Visas and Immigration)
- LANGUAGECERT: its International ESOL, Academic or General SELT
- Pearson: Pearson Test of English (PTE) Academic UKVI
- PSI Services (PSI): Skills for English UKVI
- Trinity College London: Integrated Skills in English (ISE) or Graded Examinations in Spoken English (GESE)

For one of them we have the score. The [IELTS page on tests for UK visas](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-tests-for-uk-visas-and-immigration) says that for visas needing B2 you need at least 5.5 in each of the four components of IELTS for UKVI: Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. We read it on 21 November 2026. It also says UK Visas and Immigration accepts only IELTS for UKVI tests taken in person at an IELTS for UKVI test centre.

We did not read the score tables for the other providers, so we give no figure for them. Use the approved list on the gov.uk page, which gives the level and grade for each test, and check it on the day you book. Test fees are set by the test provider, so check its booking page.

## Settlement and B2 from 26 March 2027

Here we report only what we read. The statement of changes to the Immigration Rules published as HC (House of Commons) 1691 on 5 March 2026 is on gov.uk, and we read its [accessible text](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1619-5-march-2026/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1691-5-march-2026-accessible-version) on 21 November 2026. Under the heading for the Skilled Worker appendix, it adds a paragraph headed English language requirement for settlement as a Skilled Worker. For an application made on or after 26 March 2027, the paragraph asks for B2 in speaking and listening, unless an exemption applies.

That is the wording of a published change. Rules can be amended again, and the text does not tell you how it applies to your own dates. If you expect to apply for settlement, read the current gov.uk guidance for your route before you plan lessons or book a test.

## Planning your lessons before the test

Your starting point, how often you practise, how much time you can spend outside lessons and how comfortable you are with writing all change the answer, so we do not give a figure.
