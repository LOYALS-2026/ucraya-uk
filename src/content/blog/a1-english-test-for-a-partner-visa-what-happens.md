A1 is the first level on the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR): everyday expressions and simple answers about yourself. For a partner visa, the test is a Secure English Language Test (SELT) that checks only speaking and listening, taken with an approved provider. Trinity's A1 test, for example, is a 6 minute conversation with an examiner.

## What A1 means

Trinity College London's page for its A1 test says that a person at A1 can understand everyday expressions, introduce themselves and answer questions about their lives, and communicate with others in simple phrases. That is the target: not perfect grammar, but short, clear answers to simple questions.

The gov.uk guidance on [family visas and knowledge of English](https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa/knowledge-of-english) says that if you need a test, you must pass at least level A1 for your first visa application, and that you can choose to take a higher level test. It also explains that you may have to pass a higher level if you stay in the UK after 2.5 years, and that what you need then depends on the level you passed first. We read the page on 21 November 2026. The rules can change, so read it again on the day you book.

## The speaking and listening format

The gov.uk page on [secure English language tests](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) says that for partner routes you take a test that assesses speaking and listening. There is no reading or writing in it. The test must be on the approved list, taken with an approved provider, and awarded in the two years before you apply.

Providers each have their own format. Trinity's [A1 test page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/A1-GESE-grade-2) describes GESE (Graded Examinations in Spoken English) Grade 2 as a one-to-one test of 6 minutes, in which you have a conversation with the examiner. The page adds that examiners work remotely by video conference, and that at a Trinity centre you use a tablet device set up by a steward. The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) website lists a pass in IELTS for UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) Life Skills A1 as another test for this level, which assesses listening and speaking. Compare the providers on the gov.uk page before you choose.

Trinity's page lists the fee for its SELT GESE exams as £160, with an optional Exam Protect add-on at £35 that lets you book one retake and reschedule once. Fees change, so confirm the figures on the [Trinity page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/A1-GESE-grade-2) when you book.

## Who may not need the test

Not everyone has to take a test. The gov.uk family visa page lists people who do not need to prove their knowledge of English, including applicants who are children, adults coming to be cared for by a relative, people over 65, people with a physical or mental condition that prevents them from meeting the requirement, and people who have been on a family visa in the UK for 5 years and are extending as a partner or parent. It also lists some nationalities. The list changes, so read the page and do not rely on this summary.

The same page says you can prove your English in other ways, such as with a degree from a UK institution, or with a degree taught in English from an institution outside the UK, which needs an assessment by Ecctis. It also says that if you proved your English in a previous successful visa application, you may not have to prove it again. We cannot tell you which of these applies to you. Check your own case on gov.uk.

## Booking and ID

You book with the provider, not with us. The gov.uk guidance says you should be able to take a test within 28 days of booking, though not always at the location nearest to you.

Identity checks are strict. The guidance says the details you use to book must be the same as on your passport or other identity document, and that you must show an original, valid document with your photo. A passport is the usual choice, and the page lists the others. If your surname has changed, the guidance explains what to do before you book. Read that section before you pay, because a mismatch can stop you from sitting the test.

Alongside the test, check whether any of your documents are in Bulgarian. ## Simple practice at home

You can practise A1 speaking without a teacher. Here is a made up A1 practice card of ten questions, covering the topics of name, family, home and daily routine. Say each answer aloud in a full sentence, then record yourself and listen back.

1. What is your name?
2. How do you spell your family name?
3. Where are you from?
4. Where do you live now?
5. Do you live in a house or a flat?
6. Who do you live with?
7. Do you have brothers or sisters?
8. What time do you get up?
9. What do you eat for breakfast?
10. What do you do in the evening?

This card is only a practice tool. The format, the length and the questions differ by provider, so read your provider's page. For the listening part, ask a friend to read the questions out of order, and answer without looking at the card.
