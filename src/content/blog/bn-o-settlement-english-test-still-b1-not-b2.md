Trinity College London, a test provider, reports that British National (Overseas) (BN(O)) applicants for settlement stay at B1 speaking and listening when most other settlement applicants move to B2 from 26 March 2027. That is the provider's notice, not the rule itself, so confirm your level on gov.uk before you book anything.

## Why older B2 headlines are out of date

Some of the reporting you may have seen suggested that B2 English would apply to everyone who settles in the UK. B1 is an intermediate level of English and B2 is the next level up, defined by the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). The step from one to the other matters a great deal if you are planning a test.

Headlines about a proposal are not the same as the rules in force. Proposals change after consultation, and the final wording is published later in the Immigration Rules. If a headline is older than the most recent Statement of Changes, treat it as background, and read the current gov.uk page instead.

This guide does not give immigration advice. It tells you what three pages say, shows you where we read them, and tells you how to check your own position.

## What the government papers and campaigners reported in March 2026

On 5 March 2026 the Home Office published House of Commons paper (HC) 1691, a Statement of Changes to the Immigration Rules. We read its [accessible version on gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1619-5-march-2026/statement-of-changes-to-the-immigration-rules-hc-1691-5-march-2026-accessible-version) on 21 November 2026. In many routes it sets B1 for applications made before 26 March 2027 and B2 for applications made on or after that date. In its section on the Hong Kong BN(O) route we found changes to the name of one sub-route and to the settlement application forms, and no B2 wording.

The campaign group Hong Kong Watch wrote about this the next day. We read its [post of 6 March 2026](https://www.hongkongwatch.org/all-posts/2026/3/6/bno-visa-not-impacted-by-home-office-changes-to-language-requirement-for-settlement) on 21 November 2026. A campaign group is not the government, so treat the post as a report. According to the post:

- B2 starts on 26 March 2027 for some groups who now need B1, a year later than proposed.
- An explanatory memorandum released with the changes listed the groups affected, and the BN(O) visa was not on the list.
- The group read this as a sign that BN(O) holders could be exempt from the B2 requirement, and it said it would keep arguing for B1.
- Further changes to English requirements for settlement could follow, depending on the government's assessment of responses to its consultation on earned settlement.

That last point is why you should keep checking. The post is from March 2026, and later announcements may have changed things.

Whatever the table says, confirm on gov.uk before you book. Rules can change after this page was written, and we cannot know your circumstances.

## What the B1 test actually is

The Trinity [B1 test page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/B1-GESE-grade-5) describes a Secure English Language Test (SELT) called Graded Examinations in Spoken English (GESE) Grade 5. We read the page on 21 November 2026. A SELT is an English test approved for immigration purposes. The page describes a one-to-one speaking and listening test of about 10 minutes, with an examiner, in two parts:

1. Topic: you choose a topic to prepare in advance and discuss it with the examiner. You complete a topic form before the test.
2. Conversation: a short talk with the examiner about two subject areas the examiner chooses.

The page also says that Trinity examiners deliver the test remotely by video conference, and that at a Trinity SELT centre you take it on a tablet set up by a steward. Other providers may offer a B1 test for your route, so check the options on gov.uk.

Test fees go to the test provider, not to us. Look at the provider's page on the day you book for the current fee and the booking steps.

## How to check before you book

An English test is only one part of any settlement application. The gov.uk [BN(O) visa page](https://www.gov.uk/british-national-overseas-bno-visa), which we read on 21 November 2026, says that after five years of living in the UK you can apply to live in the UK permanently. It does not set out an English level on that page. For any other requirement, such as documents, fees or timing, use the gov.uk guidance for the BN(O) route and ask a regulated immigration adviser if you are unsure.

Do these steps in order. Write the date next to each one.

1. Open the gov.uk BN(O) visa guidance and find the settlement section for your route.
2. Write down the English level it asks for, and the date you read it.
3. Check that the test you want is on the approved list for your route.
4. Look at the provider's booking page for the test dates and fee.
5. Check the level again in the week you book, because the date you read it matters.

If gov.uk and a provider's notice ever disagree, gov.uk is the rule.
