First read your offer letter, because the university's condition decides what counts, not the visa minimum. Then ask admissions what they accept: a retake of one skill, a new full test, or another route. Work back from the date your university needs your score to issue the Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS).

## Read the exact wording of your offer condition

An offer letter often asks for a score in International English Language Testing System (IELTS) terms, for example 6.5 overall with no skill below 6.0. Copy the condition word for word. Note four things: the overall score, any minimum for each skill, the test type, and the date by which the university needs the result.

A CAS is a document from your course provider. The gov.uk page on [documents you must provide](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/documents-you-must-provide) lists it among the things you need for a Student visa application (checked on 21 November 2026). So a missing English score can hold up the document that holds up your visa. That is why the date matters more than the score alone.

If your result is below the condition, write to the admissions or international office at once. Ask whether they accept a retake of one skill, whether there is a later date for the English condition, and whether there is another way to meet it. Their reply, in writing, is your guide. We cannot speak for any university.

## IELTS Academic or IELTS for UKVI: which test your offer needs

IELTS has several versions. IELTS for UKVI (UK Visas and Immigration) is the secure version that the Home Office accepts for some routes. The [IELTS page for UK visas](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-tests-for-uk-visas-and-immigration) lists, for a Student visa at degree level or above, the standard IELTS or IELTS for UKVI, with at least 5.5 in each of the four components (Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking). For study below degree level it lists IELTS for UKVI with at least 4.0 in each component. We read that page on 21 November 2026. The page itself tells you to check the details on the government website or with the organisation receiving your result.

The gov.uk [knowledge of English page](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/knowledge-of-english) gives the levels in the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR): equivalent to B2 for degree level or above, and B1 below degree level. It also says that if you study at degree level with a Higher Education Provider, the provider can assess your English itself, so it may ask you to take a different test, which must still be equivalent to B2. We read that page on 21 November 2026 too.

So the test named in your offer letter is the one to book. If it says IELTS for UKVI, a standard IELTS result may not satisfy the university. If you are unsure, ask admissions before you pay.

## The Home Office minimum is not always the university score

Many people see 5.5 in each skill and think that is their target. It may not be. Your university can set a higher overall score, a higher score in one skill, or both, and it is the university's condition that you must meet to receive the CAS.

Keep the two apart on one sheet of paper.

| | What it is | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| University condition | The score in your offer letter | Your offer letter and the admissions team |
| Visa English level | The level the visa route asks for | The gov.uk and IELTS pages above, read on the day |

Aim for the higher of the two for every skill, and check the visa rule again close to your application date.

## One Skill Retake: when it can help and when it cannot

The IELTS page on [One Skill Retake](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/booking-your-test/one-skill-retake) was read on 21 November 2026. According to the page, you can retake one of the four skills instead of the whole test. The page lists these conditions:

- Your full test must have been an IELTS on computer test, taken at a centre that offers One Skill Retake.
- You must sit the retake within 60 days of your original test day.
- You can retake only once per original test.
- You receive a new Test Report Form with the new score for that skill and your original results for the other three. You choose whether to use the original or the new form.
- Results come within 3 to 5 days, according to the page.
- Prices vary, so ask your test centre.

For UKVI, the page lists One Skill Retake as accepted for IELTS Academic and General Training tests on computer in selected locations. On acceptance, it tells you to check with the organisation directly before booking.

That last point is the key one. One Skill Retake can help when exactly one skill is below the condition and your university has agreed to accept it in writing. It cannot help when two or more skills are short, when the 60 days have passed, or when your university wants a full new test. Acceptance depends on the university, so ask first.

## Working backwards from the CAS deadline

Start with the date your university needs your result and count backwards. Leave a gap at each step in case something runs late. This is a made up example, with made up dates and scores, to show the shape of the plan.

Dana has an offer needing IELTS 6.5 overall with no skill below 6.0. Her university needs the English condition met by 1 August, so that it can issue her CAS. Her first test result was 6.0 overall, with 5.5 in writing. Only one skill is short, so she asks admissions whether they accept One Skill Retake, and they say yes in writing. Her original test day was 1 June.

| Step | Made up date | Note |
|---|---|---|
| First test day | 1 June | Original computer test |
| First result arrives | Check with your test centre | Waiting times differ, so ask the provider |
| Ask admissions about a retake | The day the result arrives | Keep their reply in writing |
| Last day to sit One Skill Retake | 31 July | 60 days after 1 June, so do not leave it to the last day |
| Planned retake day | About 15 July | Leaves a safety gap |
| Retake result | 3 to 5 days later, per the IELTS page | Allow a little extra |
| Send the new Test Report Form to the university | Straight away | Before 1 August |
| CAS request date | 1 August | University issues the CAS when the condition is met |

## A realistic lesson plan and where tutors fit

Lessons can help you practise the weakest skill, but we cannot promise a score. Use the time between your result and your next test date in three stages.

1. In week one, look at your skills report and work out why the weak skill lost marks. For writing it is often task response, organisation or grammar range, not vocabulary alone.
2. In the middle weeks, practise that skill under timed conditions and get corrections you can act on. Write, then review, then rewrite.
3. In the last week, do a full practice session in the same order as the real test, so test day feels familiar.
