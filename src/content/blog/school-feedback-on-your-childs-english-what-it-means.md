It usually means the teacher has noticed that some school English is hard for your child, such as reading, speaking in class or writing. Ask the school which part, what help is already given and how progress will be shown. Many pupils learn English as an additional language (EAL), and the feedback is a starting point for a conversation.

## What the feedback may actually be saying

School notes are short and full of shorthand. A phrase that sounds worrying can simply mean "this part of the work needs more practice". It rarely says how serious the problem is, so do not guess. Write down the exact words, then ask the teacher to explain them with an example from your child's own work.

Below is a made up example of a note a teacher might write, with a plain English meaning for each phrase. Real notes differ from school to school, so treat this as a guide to reading, not a dictionary.

| Phrase in the note | What it may mean |
|---|---|
| "Needs support with academic vocabulary" | Your child knows everyday words but finds the words used in lessons, such as "compare" or "evidence", harder |
| "Reluctant to speak in front of the class" | Your child may understand more than they say and needs confidence |
| "Struggles to organise written work" | Ideas are there, but the order of sentences or paragraphs is not clear |
| "Reading comprehension is below expectations" | Your child can read the words but misses some of the meaning |
| "Would benefit from practice at home" | The school would like more reading or speaking outside lessons |
| "Making steady progress" | Your child is improving, at a pace the teacher judges to be reasonable |

## EAL support in school: what to ask the teacher

You can ask the school for a short meeting. Take the following ten questions, which are a parent question sheet you can print or copy. They are about the school's own support, so the answers will be specific to your school.

1. What exactly did you notice that led to this feedback?
2. Which of reading, writing, speaking and listening is the main concern?
3. What support does my child receive now, and who gives it?
4. How often does the support happen, and does it take place in class or outside it?
5. What does progress look like, and how will you show it to me?
6. When will we meet again to review how things are going?
7. Is there anything my child finds difficult in particular subjects, such as maths word problems?
8. What can I do at home that fits with what you are doing in school?
9. Would it help to speak or read in our home language as well as in English?
10. Who should I contact if I am worried before the next meeting?

Ask for the answers in writing if you can, even as a short email. It helps when you compare notes later. The Bell Foundation website is one place to look for more EAL material. We have not reviewed it for this guide, so read it with the same questions in mind.

## Reading a school report for English clues

A school report can give you more information than a single note. Look for three things.

First, look for a comment on speaking and listening, because a child who follows spoken lessons well but struggles to write needs a different kind of help from one who struggles to understand. Second, look at the subjects. If the concern appears in every subject, it is likely to be about English. If it appears in one, the cause may be the words that subject uses. Third, look at the wording over time. If you can, compare this report with the last one. A phrase that has stayed the same for two terms tells you something that one report alone does not.

Do not worry if the report has no scores for English. Ask the teacher what the comments are based on, such as classwork, tests or conversation in class.

## When extra one-to-one lessons can help

Extra lessons are your choice. They can be useful when your child needs more speaking time than a busy class can give, or when one skill, such as writing a clear paragraph, needs patient practice. A tutor can also work on the topics your child is studying, so the practice connects to school.

At Ucraya, [one-to-one lessons for children](/courses/kids/) are private 55 minute lessons on a live video call, at £15 per lesson, or £20 per lesson when the lesson is taught entirely in English. You agree the timetable with the tutor. We cannot promise how fast your child will improve, because it depends on the child, the age and how often they practise.

Lessons are not the answer when the cause is something else. If your child seems unhappy at school, tired, anxious or struggling in every subject, talk to the teacher first. Lessons also add to the workload of an already full week, so ask the teacher whether your child has the energy for them before you book.

## Safeguarding questions to ask any online tutor

Before a child has an online lesson, ask the provider to explain, in writing if possible, how they look after children. At Ucraya a parent or carer should be present or nearby during the lesson. These are fair questions for any provider, including us:

- Who will teach my child, and what are their teaching qualifications?
- Can I stay in the room, or nearby, during the lesson?
- How do you share the lesson link, and who else can join?
- Are lessons recorded, and if so, who can see the recording?
- What should I do if something in a lesson worries me, and who will I speak to?

If an answer is vague, ask again. A provider who is comfortable with these questions will not mind them.

## A calm first step plan for the next two weeks

You do not have to decide everything at once. This plan takes about two weeks.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| Days 1 to 2 | Copy the exact words of the feedback and read them with your child, in a kind way |
| Days 3 to 5 | Book a short meeting and take the ten questions |
| Days 6 to 8 | Write down what the school will do and when you will meet again |
| Days 9 to 12 | Add ten minutes of reading or talking in English at home, using a book or topic your child likes |
| Day 14 | Decide whether you want extra lessons, and if so, tell us your goal and we will suggest a tutor and a first lesson |
