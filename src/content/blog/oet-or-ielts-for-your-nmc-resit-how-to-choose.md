If you want to add your new result to the old one, retake the same test. The Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) page says you cannot combine an Occupational English Test (OET) result with an International English Language Testing System (IELTS) result. Switching tests means starting again, so decide from the scores you hold now.

## Why you cannot combine OET and IELTS

We read the NMC page on [accepted English language tests](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/accepted-english-language-tests/) on 21 November 2026. The page lets you combine scores across two sittings, but only for tests of the same type, for example two IELTS tests or two OET tests. An IELTS test cannot be combined with an OET test.

The page lists other conditions for combining. The two test dates must be within 12 months of each other, and both must be no more than two years before the NMC assesses your complete application. You must be tested in all four skills each time, and each required score must be reached in at least one of the two sittings. The NMC's test combining calculator is the place to check real results.

This guide does not give advice on your registration. It reads the NMC pages and shows you how to use them. The NMC decides what it accepts, and rules can change, so check the page on the day you book.

## What each test looks like for a nurse

The pages we read give these lines. The comparison table below has no fee figure, because we did not read a current fee.

| | OET | IELTS Academic |
|---|---|---|
| Setting the NMC describes | Healthcare | Academic |
| NMC line for listening, reading and speaking | Grade B (350 or above) | 7 |
| NMC line for writing | Grade C+ (300 or above) | 6.5 |
| Versions the NMC accepts | OET on Paper, OET on Computer and OET@Home, Nursing version | Paper or computer at a test centre, and IELTS Academic UKVI (UK Visas and Immigration) |
| Versions it does not accept | Not listed on the page | IELTS Indicator, IELTS Online, IELTS One Skill Retake |
| Combining rule | Only with another OET result | Only with another IELTS result |
| Fee | Check the provider on the day you book | Check the provider on the day you book |

For OET the NMC page tells you to choose the Nursing version of the test. Healthcare tasks may feel familiar if you work on a ward. IELTS Academic uses academic topics instead, so some nurses find the content less close to their work. Neither point decides the matter alone.

## Read your scores against the combining rules

This is the step most people skip. Your last score report may already tell you which test to sit. The NMC page sets a floor score for each skill in each sitting, below the lines above. For IELTS the floors are 6.5 for listening, reading and speaking and 6 for writing. For OET they are grade C+ (300 to 340) for listening, reading and speaking and grade C (250 to 290) for writing. Every score in a sitting has to reach the floor for that sitting to be used in a combination.

Here are three made up examples, with invented numbers. None of these people is real.

| Made up example | Result | What the rules point to |
|---|---|---|
| Mei, IELTS Academic | Listening 7, reading 7.5, writing 6, speaking 6.5 | All scores reach the floors. She needs writing of 6.5 and speaking of 7 in a second IELTS sitting that also reaches every floor. Retaking IELTS keeps her listening and reading. |
| Tomas, OET | Listening 340, reading 360, writing 280, speaking 350 | All scores reach the floors. He needs listening of 350 and writing of 300 in a second OET sitting. Retaking OET keeps his reading and speaking. |
| Ravi, IELTS Academic | Listening 6, reading 7, writing 7, speaking 7 | Listening is under the 6.5 floor, so this sitting cannot be used in a combination. He starts fresh, and the choice of test is open. |

The same logic applies to anyone: if your sitting reaches every floor and only one or two skills are short, staying with the same test lets you keep the good scores. If a score is under a floor, the old sitting is no help, and you can weigh the two tests more freely.

Do not rely on this table for your own case. Put your real scores into the NMC calculator.

## Which to choose if you missed one part

If you missed one part by a small margin and your other scores reach the floors, the quickest path on paper is a resit of the same test, preparing only for the part you missed. You still sit all four skills again, because the NMC page says you cannot take or retake single skills. A strong score on the day in your weaker skill is what you need, and the other three must not fall below their floors.

If you are thinking of switching, ask what you gain. Some nurses switch because the next OET date is sooner than the next IELTS date, or because the tasks suit their work. That can be a good reason. Just see it clearly: your old result stops counting towards a combination, so you must reach every NMC line in the new test, in one sitting or across two sittings of that new test.

Also look at the clock. Your two sittings must be within 12 months, and both must be within two years of the NMC assessment. A plan with a gap of a year between tests can run into that limit.

## Fees: read them on the day

We did not read a fee for either test, so this guide gives no figure. Fees change, and they vary by test type, place and date. On the day you book, open the test provider's page and note the price of the exact version the NMC accepts. IELTS Academic UKVI, for example, is a different version from the standard test, and the NMC accepts both. Then add the cost of a possible second sitting, so you can decide what a resit plan costs in total.
