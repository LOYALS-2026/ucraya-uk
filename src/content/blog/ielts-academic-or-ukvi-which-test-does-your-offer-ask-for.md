Look for the exact test name in your offer letter, then ask admissions in writing. The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) has an Academic version for study and an IELTS for UKVI version for some visa routes, where UKVI means UK Visas and Immigration. Your university and your visa decide which one you book.

## Where the test type is written in your offer

Open your offer letter or the conditions in your applicant portal and find the English language line. Copy it word for word into a note. Look for three details: the name of the test, the overall score, and any minimum score in a single skill such as writing.

The name matters because the tests have different names even though they look similar. "IELTS", "IELTS Academic", "IELTS for UKVI" and "IELTS for UKVI Academic" can mean different things on a booking page. If the wording only says "IELTS" with a score, do not guess. Ask.

If the offer lets you meet the English condition in another way, such as a pre-sessional course, that is a separate conversation with the university. This guide is about the case where you need a test result.

## What IELTS for UKVI is and who needs it

The IELTS website explains that [IELTS for UKVI](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-tests-for-uk-visas-and-immigration) is the family of tests accepted by UK Visas and Immigration as proof of English for people who want to live, work or study in the UK. It adds that UKVI only accepts tests taken in person at an official IELTS for UKVI test centre, because UKVI requires an additional layer of security.

The same page lists the score for a Student visa, with a note that the information was collected from the government website and the reader should confirm with gov.uk or the receiving organisation. We read the page on 21 November 2026.

| Student visa level | Language level on the IELTS page | Test and score on the IELTS page |
|---|---|---|
| Below degree level | B1 | IELTS for UKVI, at least 4.0 in each of the four parts |
| Degree level or above | Equivalent to B2 | IELTS or IELTS for UKVI, at least 5.5 in each of the four parts |

The levels B1 and B2 come from the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). These are the Home Office levels, and a university can ask for more. Your offer is the number to plan around, and the gov.uk page for your visa is the one to check on the day.

## IELTS Academic, and why General Training needs a question first

The IELTS website describes [Academic and General Training](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types) as two versions with the same Speaking and Listening sections and different Reading and Writing sections. It describes Academic as the likely choice for study in an English speaking country, including university and higher education, and General Training as suitable for migration and for training or study below degree level.

So if your offer is for a degree, General Training is worth a question before you book it, not a guess. The reverse is also true: if your offer says nothing about UKVI, a standard Academic test may be what the university wants. Only the university can say.

As for difficulty, the Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) has an [IELTS Academic page](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/accepted-english-language-tests/ielts-academic/) that describes the UKVI version as the same as the standard Academic test by all other measures, with the same content, format, scoring and level of difficulty. The differences are in the booking, the test centre and the result report.

## Questions to email admissions before booking

Send one short email and keep the reply. Below is a template with three questions, followed by a made up reply from a fictional admissions office, shown only so you can see what a useful answer looks like. A real reply will use different words.

Template:

> Subject: English language condition, your name, your application number
>
> Dear Admissions Team,
>
> My offer for your course name has an English language condition. Before I book a test, could you please confirm:
>
> 1. Which IELTS test do you need: IELTS Academic, IELTS for UKVI Academic, or either?
> 2. What are the minimum scores, overall and in each skill?
> 3. Do you accept IELTS One Skill Retake if I need to improve one skill?
>
> Thank you for your help.
>
> Your name

A made up reply:

> Dear Student, thank you for your email. We need IELTS Academic or IELTS for UKVI Academic taken in the last two years, with 6.5 overall and no part below 6.0. We do not accept One Skill Retake for this course. Please send us your test report form when you have it.

In this example the office names the test, the scores and the retake position in three lines. That is what you want. If the real reply is vague, write back and ask the question again.

## The preparation is the same, but the booking is not

The lessons you need do not change with the label. Whichever version you book, you practise the same four skills, and you work hardest on the skill that is furthest from the score you need. The booking is where the labels matter: the wrong version could mean a test your university does not accept.

So the order is simple. Get the test name and scores in writing, book the right test, then plan your preparation backwards from the test date.
