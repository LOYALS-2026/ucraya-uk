The Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC) accepts the Occupational English Test (OET) and IELTS Academic (International English Language Testing System). For OET you need grade B (350 or above) in listening, reading and speaking and grade C+ (300 or above) in writing. For IELTS Academic you need 7 in listening, reading and speaking and 6.5 in writing.

## Which tests the NMC accepts

The NMC's page on [accepted English language tests](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/accepted-english-language-tests/) says it accepts two tests as evidence: IELTS Academic and OET. It adds that these are the only tests it can accept, and that it is not looking to accept any others at the moment.

The page also sets out the criteria any test must meet. The test must check English in a healthcare or academic setting and not be a general test, it must test reading, writing, speaking and listening, and a person, not a computer, must assess the speaking.

We read these pages on 21 November 2026. The NMC can change its scores and its list, so treat this guide as a pointer and check the page again on the day you book.

## OET: grade B in three parts and C+ in writing

The NMC's [OET page](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/accepted-english-language-tests/oet/) asks for at least grade B (350 or above) in reading, listening and speaking, and at least grade C+ (300 or above) in writing. Applicants should choose the Nursing version of the test. The NMC accepts OET on Paper, OET on Computer and OET@Home.

The score table below puts the OET and IELTS Academic figures side by side, as they appear on the NMC pages we read.

| Skill | OET grade the NMC asks for | OET score | IELTS Academic score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listening | B | 350 or above | 7 |
| Reading | B | 350 or above | 7 |
| Writing | C+ | 300 or above | 6.5 |
| Speaking | B | 350 or above | 7 |

Here is a made up example of how to read your own result. A nurse has an OET result of 360 for listening, 350 for reading, 340 for speaking and 310 for writing. Listening and reading meet grade B. Writing is above the C+ line of 300. Speaking, at 340, is the one part below grade B, so it is the part that stands between her and the required scores.

Combining results is possible under conditions, which are described below. The NMC has a test combining calculator, and that is the place to check a real result.

## IELTS Academic: what the NMC page says

The NMC's [IELTS Academic page](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/accepted-english-language-tests/ielts-academic/) asks for at least 7 in reading, listening and speaking and at least 6.5 in writing. It accepts the paper-based and computer-based IELTS Academic test taken at a test centre, and it accepts IELTS Academic UKVI (UK Visas and Immigration).

The page names three versions that do not count: the IELTS Indicator, IELTS Online and IELTS One Skill Retake. For One Skill Retake, the page explains that you must be tested in all four skills at the same time, and you cannot take or retake single skills separately.

## How long a result stays valid

The NMC says test scores are valid for two years. The test or tests must have been taken in the two years before the NMC assesses your complete application. Note the wording: it is the date of the test, not the date on the certificate, that counts.

If you do not reach every score in one sitting, the NMC lets you combine scores from two sittings if you meet its conditions. In summary, the page says that you must sit the two tests within 12 months of each other, take both within two years of the assessment, be tested in all four skills each time, reach each required score in at least one of the sittings, meet minimum scores in every skill in both sittings, and combine tests of the same type. You cannot combine an OET result with an IELTS result. The exact minimums are on the NMC pages, and its calculator checks your scores.

## When you may not need a test at all

A test is one of three types of English language evidence on the NMC's [English language requirements page](https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/english-language-requirements/). The other two are a pre-registration nursing, midwifery or nursing associate qualification that was taught and examined in English, and one year of recent practice as a nurse, midwife or nursing associate in a country where English is the majority spoken language.

The page also describes supporting information from employers, which some internationally educated professionals can use as part of their evidence, depending on their circumstances. We cannot tell you which route fits you. Read the NMC page for your own situation, and ask the NMC if you are unsure.
