Both are on the government's list for partner routes, so choose by format, availability and fee. Trinity's Graded Examinations in Spoken English (GESE) are one-to-one conversations with an examiner. IELTS (International English Language Testing System) Life Skills is taken with an examiner and one other test taker. Check your own route on gov.uk first.

## What approved providers have in common

The gov.uk guidance on [Secure English Language Tests](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt) (SELT) says that partner applications need a test that assesses speaking and listening. We read the page on 23 November 2026. It sets three conditions for a result to be accepted: the test must be on the approved list, it must have been sat at an approved test location, and it must have been awarded in the two years before the date of your application.

The page lists four providers for people in the UK: Trinity College London, the IELTS SELT Consortium, LANGUAGECERT and Pearson. For IELTS it names the tests 'IELTS for UKVI' (UK Visas and Immigration) and 'IELTS Life Skills', and for Trinity it names the Graded Examinations in Spoken English (GESE). The page adds that it is for you to decide which test to take.

The level comes from your route. The [family visa page](https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa/knowledge-of-english) says you must pass at least level A1 on the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) for a first application, and that after 2.5 years you may need at least A2 in speaking and listening if you passed A1. Not everyone needs a test, so read the page for your own case. Our guide to [A1, A2 or B1 and when](/blog/partner-visa-english-test-a1-a2-or-b1-and-when/) walks through the stages.

## Format compared

Here is what the provider pages we read on 23 November 2026 say. Treat the table as a summary of those pages, not a rule.

| | Trinity GESE (A1 is Grade 2, A2 is Grade 3) | IELTS Life Skills (A1 or A2) |
|---|---|---|
| Who is in the room | You and one examiner, one to one | You, an examiner and one other test taker |
| Length | 6 minutes at A1, 7 minutes at A2 | Around 20 minutes |
| What it covers | Speaking and listening conversation | One test covering speaking and listening |
| Result | Provisional result emailed by 9am the day after | Pass or Fail, not scored |

On the Trinity [A1 page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/A1-GESE-grade-2) and [A2 page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/A2-GESE-grade-3), the exam is a conversation with the examiner. At a Trinity centre it takes place on a Trinity tablet set up by a steward, with examiners joining remotely by video conference.

The [IELTS Life Skills format page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-tests-for-uk-visas-and-immigration/ielts-life-skills-test/life-skills-test-format-in-detail) says the topics are everyday ones, such as personal details, family and friends, buying goods, work, health, leisure, transport, housing and the weather. At A2 the tasks can add comparing, prioritising, planning and persuading to the A1 tasks. The [main IELTS page](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-tests-for-uk-visas-and-immigration/ielts-life-skills-test) explains that a Pass means you can proceed with your visa application and a Fail means the result cannot be used.

This is the practical difference. With Trinity, every minute is your conversation with one examiner. With IELTS Life Skills you also share the test with another test taker, so listening to them and taking your turn matters. Our guide to the [A1 test](/blog/a1-english-test-for-a-partner-visa-what-happens/) covers what happens on the day for Trinity.

## Fees as estimates

Fees change and differ by place, so these are estimates from the pages we read on 23 November 2026, each on the same line as its link. Treat any figure as a starting point and read the provider's page on the day you book.

- Trinity: the [A1 page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/A1-GESE-grade-2) lists the fee for SELT GESE exams as £160, and the A2 page lists the same.
- IELTS Life Skills A2: the [British Council Cardiff test centre page](https://ielts.org/test-centres/british-council-cardiff) listed £185.00 for Life Skills A2. The page says prices may vary and may be subject to country or local tax, and it showed no other Life Skills level.

We did not find an IELTS Life Skills fee for A1 on a page we read, so we give none. Ask the test centre you plan to use. Test fees are paid to the provider, not to us.

## Where you can sit the test

The gov.uk page says you should be able to take a test within 28 days of booking, but it may not be at the location nearest to you. Approved test locations are on lists that gov.uk publishes, and the page says to check the provider's website for dates and not to contact the test centre directly.

Trinity's pages say you take the exam at one of its SELT centres, and that Trinity has over 20 across the UK. The IELTS page says you can book in person at its approved SELT locations in the UK and across the world, and that the A2 Life Skills test is only offered in the UK. Neither page we read describes a test taken at home, so do not plan on one.

Book through the provider. The gov.uk page warns that some third party providers claim links with the Home Office when they have none, and that a third party should charge the same as, or less than, the price on the provider's website.

## Retake rules

The gov.uk page says that in certain circumstances, if you failed one component of a test, you may be allowed to sit that component again, and that you must check the provider's terms before you book. A partner test is a single speaking and listening test, so ask the provider what its retake rule is before you pay.

For Trinity, the [A2 page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/A2-GESE-grade-3) lists an add-on called Exam Protect at £35 on top of the exam fee, which lets you book one retake and reschedule once. We did not read an IELTS Life Skills retake rule on the pages above, so check the terms of your test centre.

## Check the gov.uk approved list

Before you book, work through this short list.

1. Read the family visa page for your stage and write down the level and the date you read it.
2. Open the gov.uk SELT page and check that the exact test name and level are on the approved list.
3. Choose a provider and an approved test location, and read its fee, retake terms and ID rules on its own page.
4. Book only through the provider, and keep the confirmation.
5. Check that you can reach the date and place before any deadline in your application.
