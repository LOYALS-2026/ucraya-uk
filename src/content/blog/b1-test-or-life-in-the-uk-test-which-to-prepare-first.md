They are separate tests, so you may need both. The gov.uk page for British National (Overseas) (BN(O)) settlement says people aged 18 to 64 will usually need to pass the Life in the UK Test and show English at level B1 in speaking and listening. Lessons help with the English. A book prepares you for the other test.

## Two different tests, two different skills

The first test is about your English. B1 is an intermediate level on the Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR), the scale used for language levels. The second test is about what you know of life in the UK, and your English only matters because you have to read the questions.

We read the gov.uk [BN(O) settlement page](https://www.gov.uk/british-national-overseas-bno-visa/settle-in-the-uk) on 21 November 2026. Its section on knowledge of language and life in the UK lists, for people aged 18 to 64, booking and passing the Life in the UK Test, and meeting the English requirement with a qualification at B1 or above in speaking and listening, or a degree taught or researched in English. The page adds that some people do not have to take the Life in the UK Test or meet the English requirement.

This guide does not give immigration advice. It reports what the pages say. Your own circumstances decide what you must do, so read the page for your route and, if you are unsure, ask UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) or a qualified adviser.

## What the Life in the UK test covers

We read the gov.uk [Life in the UK Test page](https://www.gov.uk/life-in-the-uk-test) on 21 November 2026. The page lists a booking rule of at least 3 days in advance online, and a test cost of £50. There are over 30 test centres in the UK and you choose one when you book.

The page says you will be tested only on information from the official Guide for New Residents. It describes 24 questions in 45 minutes about British traditions and customs. You can study from the book, an eBook, audio formats, an e-learning subscription or the official practice app.

To book you need an email address, a debit or credit card and an accepted form of ID. The page lists the accepted forms, including a share code for an eVisa (a digital record of your immigration status). The name on the booking must match the ID exactly. Check the list on the day, because the rules for older residence cards have dates attached.

The page also lists who does not need to take the test, for example people aged 65 or over, people who have passed it before and people with a long-term physical or mental condition who provide the right evidence.

## What B1 speaking and listening covers

The Trinity page on its [B1 test (GESE Grade 5)](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/B1-GESE-grade-5) was also read on 21 November 2026. Graded Examinations in Spoken English (GESE) Grade 5 is a Secure English Language Test (SELT), which means a test approved for immigration purposes. The Trinity page describes a 10 minute one-to-one conversation with an examiner, in two parts. In the first part you discuss a topic that you chose and prepared, and you bring a topic form. In the second part you talk with the examiner about two subjects the examiner picks.

The [Trinity page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/B1-GESE-grade-5) lists the exam fee as £160, and a provisional result by email the next morning. It also carries a notice from Trinity, the test provider and not the government, that most people applying to settle will need a B2 speaking and listening test from 26 March 2027, while the page lists BN(O) applicants as continuing to need B1. Our [BN(O) test guide](/blog/bn-o-settlement-english-test-still-b1-not-b2/) explains where that notice comes from and why you should confirm it on gov.uk.

Lessons are not the test. We do not run it, and you book it with a test provider.

## Which to book first

Nothing we read sets an order for the two tests, so this is a planning question and not a rule. Here are the points that tend to decide it.

- Dates: look at the next free slot for each test near you. If one has a long wait, book it first and prepare for the other while you wait.
- How much you need to learn: the Life in the UK Test needs a lot of facts. The B1 test needs speaking practice that builds up over weeks.
- Certificate age: the Trinity page says it has recommended that only certificates less than two years old be accepted for immigration use. Do not take a test too early for your application date. Check your route on gov.uk.
- Your English: if reading a long book in English feels slow, start the book early.

## Study time for each

A table first, then a plan. The columns compare the two tests as the pages describe them.

| | B1 speaking and listening | Life in the UK Test |
|---|---|---|
| Format | 10 minute conversation with an examiner | 24 questions in 45 minutes |
| Skill | Spoken English you can use in conversation | Knowledge of UK facts from one book |
| Set by | A test provider such as Trinity | The Home Office, booked on gov.uk |
| How to prepare | Speaking practice with a person who corrects you | Read the official Guide for New Residents and use practice questions |

Here is a four week plan for a made up example: Wing is not a real person, and the hours are invented to show how a plan can be split. Wing has an evening or two free each week and booked both tests for week five.

| Week | Life in the UK | B1 speaking |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Read the first chapters of the Guide, 3 hours | Two lessons: topic choice and a first conversation, 2 hours |
| 2 | Next chapters and a practice quiz, 3 hours | Two lessons: answering follow-up questions, 2 hours |
| 3 | Finish the book, then redo weak topics, 3 hours | Two lessons: a timed mock of the 10 minutes, 2 hours |
| 4 | Practice questions only, 2 hours | One lesson and a final mock, 1 hour, plus a day to rest |

The split changes with your English. If conversation is the harder part for you, move the hours from the left column to the right.
