Yes, if you held a Skilled Worker visa before 8 January 2026 and are extending or updating it. Gov.uk says you then need B1 English and do not have to prove it again. A new application needs B2, and so does switching from another visa. We read the page on 21 November 2026, so check it again before you plan.

## The date that decides it: 8 January 2026

The [gov.uk Skilled Worker page on knowledge of English](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/knowledge-of-english) uses one date to split two groups. The level depends on whether you had the visa before 8 January 2026, and on what you are applying to do now.

The level itself comes from the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR). B1 is intermediate and B2 is upper intermediate. The page says that if you need to prove your English with a Secure English Language Test (SELT), you must show you can read, write, speak and understand English to at least B2.

Below is a made up decision table of three applicants. It shows the question each person should check on gov.uk, and where the page is clear and where you should read it for yourself.

| Applicant | Situation | What the page says | What to check on gov.uk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Priya | New application from abroad | B2 in all four skills, unless an exemption or another way of proving English applies | Whether she has a UK degree or another accepted proof |
| Tomas | Had the visa before 8 January 2026 and is extending | B1, and no need to prove it again | That his last visa was held before the date |
| Grace | Switching to Skilled Worker from a different visa, such as Student | B2 | Whether any exemption applies. Check gov.uk |

Names and cases are invented, and the rules above are only what the page said on the day we read it.

## Extensions for people already on the route

The page says that if you had this visa before 8 January 2026 and you are applying to extend or update it, you need level B1 English, and you do not need to prove your knowledge of English again. Updating means a change such as a new job or employer, so read what counts as an update on gov.uk.

The page adds one more case. If you are switching from a Health and Care Worker visa, you also do not need to provide proof again. This is a rule for that route only, so do not assume it applies to other visas.

If you are not sure whether your earlier visa counts as having the visa before the date, find the start date on your visa documents and ask a regulated immigration adviser. We cannot give immigration advice.

## New applications and the B2 rule

For a new application, the page says you will usually need to prove your English when you apply. You can prove it by having a UK school qualification that you began under 18, a degree from a UK institution taught in English, a degree from outside the UK taught in English with an assessment from Ecctis, or a pass in an English test from an approved provider. It also says that if you have already proved your English in a previous successful visa application, you may not have to prove it again.

If you take the test route, B2 is the level, in four skills. The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) website lists the score for B2 UK visas as at least 5.5 in each of the four parts of IELTS for UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI). We read that page on 21 November 2026. Other approved tests exist, and the list is on the gov.uk page for [secure English language tests](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/prove-your-english-language-abilities-with-a-secure-english-language-test-selt).

The page also names people who do not need to prove their English, including nationals of some countries and some regulated professionals who have passed an assessment accepted by their professional body. The list is long and changes, so read it on gov.uk and do not rely on a summary.

Switching from another visa is the case people most often get wrong. The page says that if you apply to switch from a different visa, you need B2. If your case does not fit the examples neatly, do not guess. Check gov.uk or ask an adviser.

## Dependants

This guide is about the person applying as the main Skilled Worker. For partners and children, the gov.uk page on [your partner and children](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-partner-and-children) is the place to look. The version we read on 21 November 2026 did not mention an English requirement for them. Dependants' situations vary, and the rules can change, so read the page and the guidance for your own route, and ask an adviser if something is unclear.

## Settlement later

Plan for settlement early, because the level may differ from the one you need now. The gov.uk page on [settling in the UK](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/settle-in-the-uk) says you must be able to speak, read, write and understand English and will usually need to prove it. It does not give a level.

Trinity College London's [B1 test page](https://www.trinitycollege.com/qualifications/SELT/B1-GESE-grade-5) carries a notice about a B2 Speaking and Listening test for most settlement applications from 26 March 2027, with some routes, including Domestic Worker, staying at B1. That notice is Trinity's, and we have not checked the immigration rules behind it. Read the current gov.uk guidance before you decide which test to prepare for.
